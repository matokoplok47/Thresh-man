Texture Forge

Turns photographs of ordinary surfaces into black and white masks, colour ramps and looping clips for VJ software. Everything runs in the browser, nothing is uploaded anywhere.

The idea

Photograph texture: asphalt, potholes, bark, gravel, branches against the sky, peeling paint, puddles, rust. Any surface with contrast between light and dark works. The app reduces the photo to a mask you can layer over video in Resolume, or colourise into something that no longer looks like a photo at all.

Shoot close and flat. A pothole filling the frame beats a landscape, because the mask lives on fine detail rather than on composition.

How a mask is made

Every photo goes through the same chain. Understanding it makes the sliders obvious.

Channel
Decides what counts as bright. Luma is normal brightness. Brightest channel pulls out saturated colour. Darkest channel exaggerates silhouettes, which is why the tree presets use it: branches are dark in every channel, sky is not.
Blur
Smooths before the cut, killing single pixel noise so the mask does not fizz.
Detail boost
A high pass. Subtracts the local average, so broad lighting gradients vanish and only fine structure survives. This is what finds cracks and grain.
Black and white point
Choose which slice of the tonal range is used at all. A narrow window means harsh contrast.
Threshold and softness
Where the cut happens and how hard the edge is. Softness above zero gives anti-aliased edges instead of jagged ones.
Edge detect
A Sobel operator, blended in by the slider. At full strength you get outlines instead of filled areas, which suits branches, cracks and anything with a clear boundary. The tap distance scales with resolution so the line keeps the same thickness at any output size.
Auto levels
Reads the histogram of that photo and sets the black and white point from it. Use it on a batch shot in mixed light, otherwise a stop-motion clip flickers in brightness.

About the presets

The names are labels for the look they aim at, not detection. Nothing in the app recognises asphalt or bark. Each preset simply writes thirteen numbers into the sliders, so treat them as starting points and then tune.

Asphalt, Concrete, Gravel
Narrow bright window, small softness. Only the light stones pass, giving sparse specks on black.
Cracks, Bark
Heavy detail boost so the fine lines survive. Cracks also inverts, because a crack is dark and you usually want it white.
Trees, Leaves
Darkest channel with a low black point, built for silhouettes against sky. Leaves inverts.
Fine dust, Snow, Sparse
High threshold and strong blur, so almost everything is rejected and only the brightest points remain.
Grain, Static, Hard contrast
Maximum detail boost with tiny wave sizes, which shreds structure into noise.
Liquid, Ink blot
Wide blur and large slow waves. Structure dissolves into flowing shapes.
Kaleido, Negative, X-ray
Effect presets. Kaleido turns on quad mirroring, X-ray inverts all three colour channels before the mask is calculated.

Warp and effects

The noise warp displaces pixels with a smooth random field, so grain edges ripple instead of looking like a plain cut. Small waves with a high amount make noise, large waves smear the image like wet glass.

Ripple
Concentric waves out from the centre. Wave size sets how many rings.
Twirl
Rotation that fades out towards the edges, like a slow vortex.
Melt / drag
Bright pixels bleed downward. The closest thing here to a datamosh smear.
Pixel sort
Sorts runs of bright pixels along each row. The classic glitch look. Uses the threshold to decide what counts as a run.
Slice shift
Cuts the image into horizontal bands and offsets each one.
Echo trails
Repeats bright pixels sideways with decay, giving motion smear on a still image.
RGB split
Pulls the colour channels apart. Needs a palette or Grayscale tone, in 1-bit there is nothing to separate.

Tone and palette

1-bit is a pure two colour mask, smallest files, cleanest edges. Soft keeps anti-aliased edges and allows bloom. Grayscale skips the threshold entirely and keeps the full tonal range, which is what you want if Resolume is going to colourise it.

The palette maps that grey ramp onto up to eight colours. Darkest colour sits at black, brightest at white. Bands posterises into hard steps, the same idea as palette mode in the Resolume colourise plugin. Smooth blends between them.

The Random button builds a ramp in OKLCH rather than by nudging hex values, because equal steps in HSL do not look equal: a blue at fifty percent lightness reads far darker than a yellow at the same number. It always raises lightness monotonically from dark to light, rotates hue by one of six harmony angles from a fifty five degree sequential sweep up to a full complementary swing, and peaks saturation in the middle while dropping it at both ends. That combination is what makes Inferno readable and a hand picked rainbow not. It keeps the number of colours you chose.

Viridis, Magma, Inferno, Plasma, Cividis and Turbo are perceptually uniform scientific colour maps, which means brightness rises evenly across the ramp with no false banding. They read very cleanly on a projector. The rest are stylistic.

Clips

Photos play back one per frame and the sequence repeats until the clip is full, so it always closes on a whole cycle and loops. Frames per photo slows that down, random order stops the pattern being obvious.

GIF is the safe choice: no codec issues, carries alpha, saves properly on iPhone, and Resolume plays it. Best under about ten seconds. Video handles long clips at full resolution and is the only format that can carry an audio track. PNG sequence is the archival option for editing elsewhere.

Working without audio

You do not need a track. Tap the tempo four times, or type the BPM, then tick Free run. The three LFOs and the ramp start moving on that tempo and can drive anything in the routing list, so a single photo becomes a moving visual with nothing plugged in. The level sources stay at zero until real audio arrives, so route from the LFOs rather than from Bass in that mode.

Generators

A generator builds the picture from maths rather than from a photo. It animates by itself, never runs out of resolution, and goes through exactly the same chain as an image, so every threshold, edge, palette, trail and audio route applies to it. Add one from the Presets tab and it lands in the strip beside your photos, so a clip can cut between a pothole and a tunnel on the beat.

Rings, Tunnel
Concentric and perspective forms that pull towards the centre. Both read very well on a projector and are the obvious pair for mapping onto a round or deep object.
Spokes, Grid, Waves
Hard geometry. Spokes rotate, Grid pulses, Waves sweep across at an angle that slowly turns.
Clouds, Plasma, Interference
Soft flowing fields. Clouds is layered noise, Plasma the classic sum of sines, Interference two ripple sources beating against each other.
Cells
Cellular noise. Looks like cracked glass or dried mud and takes edge detection beautifully.
Spectrum
Frequency bars straight from the music, thirty two bands spaced logarithmically so the low end is not squeezed. Only moves while audio is playing.

Pattern density and Pattern speed live in the Adjust tab.

Playing live

Three ways to get the picture out. Fullscreen fills this screen with a clean canvas. Output window opens a separate bare window you can drag onto a projector or second display and leave running while you carry on tweaking in the main one. Either can be captured by NDI Screen Capture or OBS and sent into Resolume.

Record live output captures exactly what you perform, microphone reaction and all. That is a different thing from rendering a clip: the clip is calculated in advance and always perfect, the live take is what actually happened.

Tap tempo sets the tempo by hand when there is no analysed track, or when the detector guesses wrong. Four taps is enough. Sync downbeat restarts the LFOs on the beat you feel, which is how you get them back in phase after a drop.

With the camera running you also get Mirror, a front and back switch, and Freeze, which drops the current frame into the strip as an ordinary photo so you can collect material outdoors and work on it later.

What Mix is for

Think of it as two projectors pointed at the same wall. Layer A is whatever is selected in the strip. Layer B is a second source you pick from the list. The crossfader says how much of B reaches the wall, and the blend mode says how the two are combined once they get there.

The quickest way to understand it: add the Rings generator, select your photo in the strip, open Mix, tick the box, choose Rings as layer B, set the mode to Multiply and drag the crossfader. The rings now cut into your photo. Switch to Add and they light it up instead. Switch to Difference and you get hard electric edges where the two disagree.

Why it matters for a live set: without it, changing source is a hard cut. With auto pilot on, every beat hands over with a real transition, and the crossfader itself can be driven by the bass so the second layer breathes in and out on its own.

Two layers

The Mix tab renders a second source underneath and blends the two. Layer A is whatever is selected in the strip, layer B you pick from the list, and the crossfader moves between them. Twelve blend modes are available: Dissolve, Add, Screen, Multiply, Difference, Lighten, Darken, Wipe, Blinds, Noise dissolve, Slide and Hard cut. Add and Screen are the ones that build up light, Multiply and Darken carve it away, Difference gives the hard electric edges.

Auto pilot hands over to the next source on the beat with a real transition instead of a cut, the same idea as the auto pilot on a VJ deck. Transition length sets how long the fade takes, and the crossfader itself is a routing destination, so bass can ride the fader by hand while the auto pilot does the swapping.

Blending renders both layers, so it costs roughly three times a single one. On this laptop two layers at 480x270 take about 9 ms against 3 ms for one.

Audio sources

Ten things can drive the visuals. Beat fires on a detected hit and then falls away. Bass is 20 to 160 Hz, the kick and the low end. Mid is 160 Hz to 2 kHz, where most of the body of the music sits. High is 4 to 12 kHz, hats and cymbals. Level is everything at once. On top of those, three LFOs and a Ramp run locked to the tempo rather than to the sound: one cycle per bar, per half bar and per beat. They give perfectly regular motion that never misses, which is what you want for slow breathing or a rotating pattern underneath the reactive parts. They follow the BPM detected from a loaded track, or the manual BPM slider when there is none. The meter in the Audio tab shows all five moving in real time, which is the fastest way to find out which one actually has life in your track.

Reaction presets

They stay pressed and combine. Motion presets only touch movement, Look presets only touch brightness, bloom and colour, so the two groups never fight. Press one from each and you get both at once; press again to switch it off. If two presets in the same group reach for the same destination, the one pressed later wins that line.

Shaping the reaction

Raw numbers from an analyser jitter, so three controls sit between the sound and the picture.

Sensitivity
How easily a moment counts as a beat. If nothing triggers, turn it up. If everything triggers, turn it down.
Attack
How quickly a value climbs when the sound arrives. A low value snaps instantly and feels percussive; a high value eases in and feels like a swell.
Release
How long the value stays up afterwards. Short gives sharp separated stabs, long gives slow breathing. For a clear pulse keep Release shorter than the gap between beats, which at 128 BPM is 470 ms. Above about a second the value never comes back down and everything looks static, which is the usual reason it seems to do nothing.
Noise gate
Discards everything below the threshold so quiet passages stop shimmering. Levels are also normalised against a slowly falling peak, so a quiet track will not look dead.

Audio

Nine destinations can each be driven by one of five audio sources: the beat envelope, bass, mid, high or overall level. The amount runs from minus to plus, so a source can push a parameter either way. Zoom gives the breathing pulse, Melt the downward drip, Shift a sideways slide, and Slice glitch the torn bands.

The detector shapes those sources before they reach anything. Attack sets how quickly a value rises, Release how slowly it falls, and the noise gate discards everything underneath it so quiet passages stop shimmering. Levels are also normalised against a slowly decaying peak, which keeps a quiet track from looking dead and a loud one from pinning at maximum.

A loaded track is analysed up front rather than while playing, so the exported clip is locked to the music and does not depend on how fast your device is. Detected beats advance the photo, bass moves the threshold, highs drive glitch and overall level drives bloom.

Live microphone input reacts on the spot for rehearsal. On iPhone the mic only works in a normal Safari tab, never in an app added to the home screen, which is an iOS restriction and not something this app can work around.

Into Resolume

With alpha, drop the layer in and use Alpha or Multiply. Without alpha, use Add or Screen. Grayscale plus the Colorize plugin gives you live control over the palette instead of baking it in here.

For a live feed, use Fullscreen live output in the Audio tab to get a clean canvas with no interface, then capture that window with NDI Screen Capture or OBS and send it to Arena as a source. A browser cannot emit NDI directly, it has no access to raw network sockets.

Keyboard

Live camera

The camera becomes just another item in the strip, so every slider, preset, palette and audio route applies to it live. Point it at a surface, a hand, a face, anything, and it is processed the same way a photo would be. Combine it with Trails and a high Edge detect and the outlines flow around whatever moves in front of the lens.

Same iOS rule as the microphone: the camera only works in a normal Safari tab, never in an app added to the home screen.

Trails

Trails keep a fading copy of every frame and push it along a slow noise current, so bright shapes leave liquid streaks that curl around them rather than simply fading in place. Persistence sets how long a streak survives, Flow speed how fast the current drags it, and Flow scale how large the swirls are. Nothing accumulates on a still image, so use it with the camera, inside a clip, or with audio driving the picture. Route Trails and Flow speed from the beat and the streaks surge on every hit.

Handy to know

Undo in the header, or the Z key, steps back through the last three changes, including deleting a source, reordering the strip and adding a generator. Double tap or double click any slider to send it back to its default. Press and hold a photo in the strip to drag it into a new order. The ADV button in the header reveals the advanced sliders, and i hides these explanations once you no longer need them.

Save a look with "Save current look as a preset" in the Presets tab. Long press or right click one of your own presets to delete it. Saved presets only last for the session, so use Export to write them to a file and Import to bring them back, on this device or another one.

Limits worth knowing

Clips can run to sixty seconds on mobile and five minutes on desktop, but a long GIF gets very heavy, so use Video for anything past about ten seconds. Clip resolution drops automatically when many frames have to be held at once, and the app reports the size it settled on. iOS cannot play WebM. Apple Music files are protected and cannot be loaded, use a plain MP3, M4A or WAV. HEIC photos may not decode on desktop browsers.
